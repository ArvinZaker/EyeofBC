Medical Student Learning

Eye complaints are encountered across family medicine, emergency medicine, pediatrics and internal medicine; trainees have limited chances to practise ophthalmic examination or learn accessible care for patients with vision loss.

Caring for Patients With Vision Loss

  1. Introduce yourself clearly (name, role, who else is present).
  2. Ask before offering assistance.
  3. Sighted-guide technique (ask, offer arm above the elbow, walk slightly ahead at comfortable pace, describe steps/obstacles, help person locate the chair; follow the person's instructions).
  4. Describe the clinical environment; say before touching; say when leaving.
  5. Speak directly to the patient.
  6. Make information accessible (large print, accessible electronic documents, screen-reader formats, audio, braille if requested, verbal explanation).
  7. Vision loss is a spectrum; ask about functional vision.
  8. Recognize rehabilitation (low-vision rehab, orientation and mobility, assistive technology, occupational therapy, workplace accommodations, peer support).
  9. Consider accessibility each encounter (written instructions, medications, follow-up, transport).
  10. Remember the individual.

Key takeaway: good care includes ensuring patients can access, understand and participate in their healthcare.

Ophthalmology Examination Skills

visual acuity and pinhole; pupils incl. RAPD; confrontation visual fields; extraocular movements; direct ophthalmoscopy; intraocular pressure; urgent red flags. Complements supervised training.
